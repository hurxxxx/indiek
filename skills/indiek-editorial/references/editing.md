# Editorial decisions

## Reference analysis

Record the title, writer, original URL, publication date, authorship/reporting evidence, checking date, format and reader promise. Describe what the opening does, how evidence advances the argument, where the author explains rather than lists, and how the piece ends. Note what must not be copied.

For reception, record the exact public response URL and only visible observations. Distinguish `positive-response`, `craft-only` and `unverified`. Cached/search-indexed counts need that label and are not live measurements. A thoughtful reply is useful qualitative evidence, not proof of broad popularity. Album ratings are not article ratings. Compare within a publisher/platform when comparable observations exist; do not invent a denominator or engagement rate.

## Fact review

Check each factual clause against opened originals. Cover dates, artist identity, credits, recording versus upload dates, translations and quotations. Treat an interviewee's account as an attributed account. Flag contradictions and inaccessible evidence. Reopen time-sensitive sources before publishing. Verify actual statements, not merely that a URL exists.

Music analysis needs supplied listening evidence with the recording identified. A title, video description or transcript does not prove how an arrangement sounds. If evidence is unavailable, remove the assertion or attribute it to a named, linked critic. Do not turn another critic's hearing into our own.

Respect source-specific quotation and summarization limits. Keep a source contribution ledger; research more originals or shorten the article when one source is carrying too much. Do not reconstruct interviews or reproduce externally sourced lyrics and translated verses. User-supplied lyrics requested for translation follow the criteria below.

## Lyric commentary

indiek's default is a calm, accessible English explanation of what the lyrics convey. Help readers understand the song through its own images, gestures and changes of perspective. Plain explanation is valuable when it clarifies something specific; literary flourish and a strong critical opinion are not required.

- Start from a meaningful detail in the verified lyrics, then explain how it supports the song's mood or theme. Summarise selectively in original prose; do not reconstruct every line or verse as a disguised translation.
- Preserve the scale of the feeling. A small wish can stay small, uncertainty can remain unresolved, and affection need not become devotion or salvation. Avoid adding drama, consolation or a universal lesson.
- Keep each inference close to its evidence. Do not invent relationship history, motives or biographical experiences. Treat the lyrical speaker separately from the performer, and attribute confirmed explanations by the songwriter. Present a plausible reading as a reading, not proof of what the writer intended.
- Use warm, clear language. Avoid abstract emotional labels in place of explanation, ornate substitute metaphors, declarations of greatness and instructions about how readers should feel. Qualify a genuinely uncertain point without making every sentence evasive.
- Let each song determine the focus and length. Do not impose the same emotional contradiction, paragraph pattern or reassuring conclusion on every note. Keep official English titles and explain a difference from the Korean title only when it affects understanding.

Review every interpretive claim for a supporting detail or attributed source. Check that the note communicates something distinctive about this lyric, preserves its ambiguity and emotional intensity, and leaves room for readers' own responses. Apply the existing fact review and exact-revision requirements. A useful, measured explanation does not need rewriting merely to sound more like a famous magazine.

## Lyric translation

Use this mode only when the user explicitly requests an English translation. Preserve the song's emotional register and concrete images in natural English. Default to a reading translation; fit a melody or rhyme scheme only when requested. For copyrighted lyrics not supplied by the user, ask for the source text instead of fetching and translating the complete lyrics. Keep commentary and translation clearly labelled.

- Read the whole supplied text before translating. Track who speaks to whom, what changes across verses, and what the refrain means on each return. Do not supply an invented relationship history.
- Match the force of the language: a tentative wish, a blunt plea and a withheld admission should remain distinct. Preserve tense, negation, uncertainty, repetition and intentional contradictions. Avoid adding consolation or dramatic intensity.
- Retain the original's important objects and metaphors. Do not replace them with a critic's elegant image or expand a small gesture into a new scene. Preserve ambiguous gender or addressees when the Korean leaves them open.
- Let English phrasing sound lived-in without adding slang or polishing away deliberate awkwardness. Preserve useful line and stanza breaks. Review repeated phrases for consistency while allowing context to change their effect.
- Add a brief note only where English cannot carry an important ambiguity or cultural nuance. Keep interpretation outside the translated lines. Preserve official English song titles and identify the text as indiek's translation, never an official translation unless it is one.

Review against the original for omissions, additions, voice, imagery and emotional intensity. Then read the English alone for natural phrasing and rhythm. Record consequential translation choices and unresolved ambiguities; apply the existing exact-revision review requirement. Literary criticism can inform attention to detail, but its metaphors and emotional conclusions must not enter the translated lyric.

## Editorial review

Judge these questions with passages and reasons, not a numerical “human” score:

- What reader question does this piece answer? Is the answer supported and useful?
- What is learned here beyond an artist biography or the source summaries?
- Does each paragraph earn its place? Are qualifications, disagreements and uncertainty preserved?
- Would replacing the artist names leave the passage largely unchanged?
- Are adjectives, grand claims and emotional instructions substituting for observations?
- Has a source's unusual metaphor, narrative order, joke or intimate memory been appropriated?
- Do the introduction and ending fit this material, or repeat recent articles?
- Does the title accurately promise what the article delivers?

“Hidden gem”, “sonic journey”, “transcends language” and “not just X but Y” often signal a missing explanation. They are revision prompts, not proof of machine authorship or automatic rejection on their own. Deliberate misspellings, forced lowercase, fake slang and random punctuation are not improvements.

## Social adaptation

Choose one detail the reader can understand without opening the article. Supply enough context to make it worthwhile, then link the relevant story. Do not turn the article into a generic teaser or withhold its basic point. Captions are not automatically article summaries; an attributed remark, a useful distinction or an unexpected documented choice can stand alone. Follow the platform guide and compare recent actual posts.

## Decision record

Return `approved`, precise issues, checked source URLs and supported claims. Editorial approval additionally records the new contribution, technique choices and comparison results. Approval binds the content hash and skill revision. If anything substantive changes, invalidate approval.

Reject a reference imitation even when factually correct. Reject a polished but empty feature even when its spelling is impeccable. Preserve a plain, well-supported paragraph when it does useful work.
